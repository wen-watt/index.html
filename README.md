Consent Mechanism: Access Constitutes Acceptance The Site does not use a pop-up "accept" checkbox to gate access to its content. This is a deliberate design choice, not an oversight, and it rests on the following reasoning: A checkbox click, in itself, does not verify that a user has actually read or understood a policy — it is a mechanical action that can be performed without attention, and its presence creates a false impression of informed consent while providing no real safeguard against inattention. The Site instead relies on the user's own affirmative, voluntary act of navigating to and browsing the Site as the operative expression of acceptance. Visiting the Site is itself a deliberate action taken by the user's own hand; no content is forced upon anyone, no data is collected before that voluntary visit, and the Disclaimer, this Policy, and the Cookie Policy are placed in plain, permanent, unobstructed view (footer link) before and during that visit. If, upon reviewing this Policy, a visitor does not agree with its terms, the appropriate and available remedy is to immediately close the Site. Because both entry and exit are actions performed entirely by the user, this symmetry — the user opens the Site by choice and may close it by choice — is treated by the Site as the clearest possible expression of informed, self-determined consent, free from the risk of accidental or inattentive agreement that a pop-up checkbox can itself introduce. This mechanism is intended to protect against exactly the failure mode that pop-up consent banners are criticized for: users reflexively dismissing a banner without reading it. By contrast, requiring the user to locate, read, and voluntarily continue past an openly published policy — with no artificial barrier or default "accept" button pre-selected for them — is the Site's chosen way of ensuring genuine, considered acknowledgment rather than a reflexive click. Jurisdictional note: this mechanism reflects the Site's own policy design and rationale. In certain jurisdictions (notably the EU/EEA, under the ePrivacy Directive as interpreted by the Court of Justice of the EU in Planet49 (C-673/17) and related EDPB guidance), regulators require a specific, informed, unambiguous, active opt-in for non-essential cookies, and passive "use implies consent" may not fully satisfy that standard regardless of the reasoning above.
